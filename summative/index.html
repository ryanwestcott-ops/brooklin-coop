Overview

What you are making.

Your presentation tells the story of your co-op in three parts:

  1. Demonstration of learning. Show the skills you learned at placement, with evidence, and how you have grown since September.
  2. Integration days. One slide each from October 7 (your goal), November 4 (your well-being toolkit) and December 2 (Skills for Success and your résumé).
  3. Looking ahead. Your next step, and advice for next year’s co-op students.

You already have most of the material: your Co-op Desk logs, your October 7 and November 4 workbooks, your Mental Health Toolkit slides and your December 2 workbook. This assignment pulls it together.

Learning goal

Reflect on and communicate what you learned through your co-op placement and integration days, using evidence, and connect it to your future pathway.

Success criteria

  • I can explain my placement, my role and at least two skills I learned, with evidence.
  • I can show how I have grown since the start of the semester.
  • I can connect each integration day to my placement experience.
  • I can explain my next step and how co-op shaped it.
  • I can present clearly to an audience in 5–7 minutes, with visuals on every slide.

Structure

Slide by slide.

The template follows this order. Section divider slides are optional. Aim for about 30–45 seconds per content slide.

  1. Introduction
  2. Title: My Co-op StoryYour name, type of workplace, your role, about how many hours you completed.
  3. My placementWhat the workplace does, your role and responsibilities, a typical day, your hours from Co-op Desk.
  4. Part 1 · Demonstration of learning
  5. Skill 1What it is and why it matters there, how you learned it, how well you can do it now, which learning-plan goal it shows. Include evidence.
  6. Skill 2Same as Skill 1, with a different skill. You can add a third skill slide if you have time.
  7. Then vs. nowYour first week compared with now: independence, new tasks you are trusted with, supervisor feedback, the biggest change in you.
  8. Part 2 · Integration days
  9. October 7 · Reflect & growThe goal you set (What), what you did and what happened (So what), your evidence, and what you learned (Now what). A goal that didn’t work out is fine if you explain what you learned.
  10. November 4 · Well-beingPaste in 1–2 favourite slides from your Mental Health Toolkit, plus an update: what is still working, and did your routine change help? Share only what you are comfortable sharing.
  11. December 2 · Skills for SuccessYour top three Skills for Success with placement evidence for each, the skill you want to keep building, your best résumé bullet, and your reference plan.
  12. Part 3 · Looking ahead
  13. My next stepA specific course, program, apprenticeship, job or career to explore. How did co-op change or confirm your plans? Your ikigai from November 4 can help. What is one step in the next three months?
  14. Advice and thanksTwo or three tips for next year’s co-op students, then thank your placement (by role or workplace type).
  15. Thank you / questionsInvite one or two questions from the audience.
Evidence

Show it, don’t just say it.

The Demonstration of learning slides need evidence. Strong evidence includes:

  • A photo of your own work: something you built, set up, repaired, organized, cooked, designed or completed.
  • A photo of you doing a task, if your workplace allows it.
  • A checklist, procedure or tool you now use on your own (blank or with private details removed).
  • Supervisor feedback: paraphrased, or quoted with permission. Your weekly sign-offs count.
  • Your Co-op Desk logs, showing a task that once needed help and later didn’t.
  • A before/after comparison.

Privacy rules

Ask your supervisor before taking any photo at work. Do it before the holiday break, because your last placement days come fast. Never include:

  • customers, clients, patients, children or coworkers who have not agreed to be shown;
  • names, records, screens, documents or anything confidential;
  • anything your workplace says is private.

If photos are not allowed, use a stock or web image of a similar task, a diagram you draw, or a written description of your work. Describe people by role (“my supervisor”, “a client”), never by name.

Presenting to the whole class is a barrier for you? Talk to Mr. Westcott early about another arrangement, such as a small group or a recorded presentation.
