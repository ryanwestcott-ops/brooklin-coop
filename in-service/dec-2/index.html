Skills for Success.

The Government of Canada’s Skills for Success are nine skills that people need in almost every job. Employers, colleges and apprenticeship programs use them. You have been building several of them at placement whether you noticed or not.

Reading

Understanding written information.

Manuals, labels, schedules, work orders, emails.

Writing

Sharing information in writing.

Logs, notes, forms, emails, signs.

Numeracy

Using numbers to get the job done.

Measuring, counting stock, cash, time, recipes.

Digital

Using technology and digital tools.

POS systems, software, scanners, apps, diagnostic tools.

Problem solving

Spotting problems and finding solutions.

Fixing an error, choosing what to do first, troubleshooting.

Communication

Listening, speaking and understanding.

Following instructions, talking with customers, asking questions.

Collaboration

Working with others toward a shared goal.

Helping coworkers, sharing tasks, handing off a shift.

Adaptability

Adjusting to change and setbacks.

A new task, a busy day, feedback, a change in plans.

Creativity & innovation

Coming up with new ideas or better ways.

A display, a faster method, a solution nobody tried.

Learn more: Skills for Success, Government of Canada ↗

Evidence makes a skill believable

“I have good communication” could be anyone. “I explain our return policy to customers and check they understand before processing the return” is you. For each skill, think: what did I actually do, and how often? Your Co-op Desk logs are full of examples.

For each skill: one specific thing you did at placement that shows it. Use your logs. This becomes your December 2 summative slide.

Résumé bullets and references.

Co-op is real work experience, so it belongs on your résumé. A strong résumé bullet follows a simple formula:

Action verb + what you did + a specific detail (how much, how often, with what, or the result).

Decide how you would improve each weak bullet, then open it to compare. These are examples. Use real details from your own placement.

Weak: “Helped customers.”

“Greeted and assisted customers on busy weekend shifts, answering product questions and directing them to the right department.”

Weak: “Did inventory.”

“Counted and recorded weekly stock for three aisles using a handheld scanner, and flagged low items for reorder.”

Weak: “Worked with kids.”

“Led a 20-minute small-group reading activity for Grade 1 students under the teacher’s supervision.”

Weak: “Fixed cars.”

“Completed tire rotations and oil changes under a licensed technician’s supervision, following the shop’s safety checklist.”

Action verbs: assisted · assembled · built · checked · cleaned · coordinated · created · delivered · designed · greeted · handled · installed · led · maintained · operated · organized · prepared · processed · recorded · repaired · served · set up · sorted · supported · trained · tracked

Be honest. Only claim what you actually did, and write “under supervision” when that is true. Leave out confidential details.

Start with your job title and workplace type, for example: “Co-op Student, Veterinary Clinic (Sept 2026 – Jan 2027)”. Then write 2–3 bullets using the formula.

Asking for a reference

A reference is someone who tells an employer what you are like at work. Your co-op supervisor may be your first work reference.

  • Ask in person, before your placement ends. Your final co-op day is January 25, so ask in early January, or before the break if it feels right.
  • Ask; don’t assume. Never list someone as a reference without permission.
  • Get their preferred contact: a work email or phone number, not a personal one, unless they offer it.
  • Say thank you, and let them know if an employer might call.

You could say: “I’ve really valued learning here. Would you be comfortable being a reference for me when I apply for jobs or programs? If so, what is the best way for an employer to contact you?”

Who would you ask (by role, not name), when, and what will you say? If your supervisor is not the right person, who else could be? Do not include anyone’s contact details here.
