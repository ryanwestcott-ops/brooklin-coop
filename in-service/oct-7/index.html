30–45 minutes · Learn

Turn experience into evidence.

A reflection explains what changed in your understanding. It goes beyond listing duties.

What?

Describe one specific situation, your responsibility and what you actually did.

So what?

Explain the result, feedback and what you learned. Connect it to a skill or course idea.

Now what?

Choose one change for next time. Explain how you will make it and what improvement will look like.

Use evidence

A concrete action, supervisor feedback or a change in the amount of help you need can show growth.

See an example

What: I rushed through a stock check and missed two items. My supervisor asked me to repeat the check with the list.

So what: I realized that speed was reducing my accuracy. Reading each line aloud to myself helped me find the missed items. This connects to attention to detail and following instructions.

Now what: For my next three stock checks, I will use the checklist and do one final comparison before reporting completion. I will ask my supervisor whether the check is accurate.

45–60 minutes · Check

Make a professional decision.

For each scenario, decide your response before opening the explanation. Then choose one to answer in your workbook.

A new task

A coworker asks you to use equipment you have not been shown how to use. They say it is easy and the supervisor is busy.

Think: What would you say? Who would you involve? Why?

Check my thinking

Pause the task and ask the supervisor for instruction and appropriate supervision. Tell your co-op teacher if the concern remains unresolved. Do not practise alone.

Feedback that stings

Your supervisor says your work needs to be more accurate. You feel embarrassed and are tempted to stop asking questions.

Think: What would you say? Who would you involve? Why?

Check my thinking

Ask for a specific example and the standard to meet. Confirm the next step, practise it and ask for feedback again. A correction can become evidence of learning.

A story worth sharing?

A friend asks who you helped at placement and wants a photo of a workplace record.

Think: What would you say? Who would you involve? Why?

Check my thinking

Keep names and records private. Discuss the skill you learned without identifying the person. Ask your supervisor about workplace information rules.

You wake up sick

You are too ill to attend placement. Your shift starts in an hour. A friend says that recording the absence in the app is enough.

Think: Who needs to know, when, and how?

Check my thinking

Before your start time, notify both your placement supervisor/employer and the school/co-op teacher using their required absence procedures. Complete the Co-op Desk absence report too, where available. An app entry does not replace telling the placement. If you cannot reach someone, use the agreed backup contact and leave a message. If an emergency prevents advance notice, report as soon as possible.

About 4–6 sentences. Include a respectful sentence you could actually say.

60–75 minutes · Discuss

Trade learning, not private details.

With a partner, take turns sharing a task you have learned and a question you still have. Each person has about three minutes; then use the remaining time to compare your experiences.

  1. Explain your task and one thing you learned.
  2. Your partner asks: “What helped you learn that?” and “What would you do next time?”
  3. Name one skill that matters in both placements.
  4. Choose one useful idea to bring into your reflection.

If you are working alone or catching up, answer the two partner questions in your notes. You can discuss ideas together; write your own answers.

Your placement reflection.

Use one real experience. Aim for clear examples rather than long answers. About 3–5 sentences for each response is a useful guide.

Name the type of workplace and your role. Describe two regular tasks without naming clients or sharing private information.

Choose one task, challenge or success. Explain the situation, your action and the result.

Compare your current skills with your first goal or starting point. Name one strength and one area for improvement, with evidence.

Classroom assignment
115–140 minutes · Plan & Submit · 25 minutes

Choose your next step.

Choose one skill you can practise within your approved duties. A strong goal names an action, a timeframe and evidence. Discuss task changes with your supervisor.

Example: “Over my next five placement days, I will repeat back multi-step instructions before starting. I will note one example in each daily log and ask my supervisor at the end of the week whether I have needed fewer reminders.”

Use: Over my next ___ placement days, I will ___ so that ___. Avoid goals such as “get better” or “work harder.”

Name two actions, when you will practise them, and the feedback or support you will ask for.

What will you observe or record? When will you review progress? Explain how you will know the goal has been met.
