ORIENTATION QUESTION CARD — DAY 5
Preparation aid only. This is not an official orientation or training record.
Ask politely; record only answers received. Mark unknowns and who will confirm them.

1. Arrival: Which entrance do I use? Where do I report, at what time, and to whom?
2. People: Who supervises me? Who is the backup if that person is unavailable?
3. Duties: What may I do, what must I not do, and what needs training or direct supervision?
4. Hazards: What could harm me in my tasks? Which controls must be in place?
5. Training: What instruction and demonstration are needed before each new task? Who checks that I can do it safely?
6. Protection: What clothing/PPE is required, who provides it, and how will I learn to use it?
7. Emergencies: What is the alarm/evacuation procedure, safe exit and meeting point? Who provides first aid? How do I summon urgent help?
8. Reporting: Who do I tell about hazards, injuries, harassment or concerns? What is the backup route?
9. Products: If relevant, where are labels/SDSs and who teaches safe product handling? Do not use an unidentified product.
10. Routines: What are the break, phone, attendance and absence procedures? How do I tell the employer and school about a delay or absence?
11. Privacy: What information must stay private? What are the rules for photographs, devices and records?
12. Learning: What safe beginner activity could support my first learning goal? How will I get feedback?

Before leaving school: confirm teacher authorization, appointment and travel/return arrangements.
Before starting placement: your teacher must confirm clearance, including required official forms and training. Do not change dates, hours or duties on your own.

CASE: CEDAR COMMUNITY CENTRE
You are preparing for a co-op role helping staff with activity supplies and reception. The fictional orientation note says:
• Report to the reception desk at your school-approved arrival time. Jordan, the program supervisor, will meet you. No backup supervisor is named.
• Initially, observe reception and help organize lightweight supplies after staff instruction. Do not access participant records, use cleaning products or operate equipment until duties, training and authorization have been confirmed.
• Bags sometimes narrow the supply-room walkway. Staff have told students to report obstructions and wait for a safe route; students should not move unknown loads.
• Staff mention an alarm and an outdoor assembly area, but do not identify the route, meeting point or first-aid contact.
• Closed-toe footwear is requested. Other task-specific protection has not been discussed.
• Staff ask you to let reception know if you will be late. The note does not replace your school reporting procedure.
• One employee says, “You can post pictures from activities to show what you learned.” No workplace or school permission has been confirmed.

TASKS (point form is fine)
1. Sort the note into at least four confirmed fictional facts and four unanswered questions. For each unknown, identify who should answer.
2. Choose three questions to ask before starting tasks, and explain your order.
3. Write the words you would use if asked to move a heavy unidentified box while Jordan is away. Explain the safe next action.
4. Explain why the employee’s photo suggestion is not enough. Propose a privacy-safe way to record learning.
