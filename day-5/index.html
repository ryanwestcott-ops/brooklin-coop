B · Orientation later

10–20 min · Everyone

Prepare before you go.

  1. Complete the workbook’s Start here section. Bring forward three questions from Days 2–4.
  2. Check appointment, travel, start arrangements, forms, training and clothing/PPE. Mark needs confirmation where appropriate.
  3. Read the Orientation Question Card. Keep a copy on your device or print it.

Official forms come from your teacher. The Work Education Agreement must be completed and signed by the relevant parties before placement. Never sign for another person, guess information or treat this workbook as an official form. Return signed documents only by the private method your teacher specifies.

The 2.5-hour minimum concerns your scheduled placement day. It does not mean today’s orientation must last 2.5 hours. Do not assume travel counts as placement hours.

20–55 min · Your assigned pathway

Do the work for your path.

A · Attend your approved orientation

Follow the appointment and arrangements agreed with your teacher. Ask questions, listen, and record what was actually explained. Do not perform unfamiliar tasks without the necessary instruction and authorization. Your actual visit may run on a different schedule from the in-school blocks.

Complete workbook Path A during or after the visit as appropriate. If the visit is cancelled or only partly completed, tell your teacher and use the assigned in-school route. Never mark an uncompleted visit as complete.

B · Prepare for your workplace

  1. Research three facts on the employer’s public website and save the source.
  2. Tailor six questions, draft an appointment message and check your paperwork status.
  3. Use the mock orientation if you need practice.

C · Build your next opportunity

  1. Find two realistic options using employer websites or Job Bank. Record sources and travel feasibility.
  2. Compare them and improve your 100–150 word introduction.
  3. Use the mock orientation if you need practice.

For B and C, keep messages as drafts until your teacher agrees to employer contact. Save work in your workbook as you go.

Reference · Use during preparation or your visit

Ask what you need to know.

This question card helps you prepare. It is not a signed orientation or training record.

  1. Arrival: Which entrance do I use? Where do I report, at what time, and to whom?
  2. People: Who supervises me? Who is the backup if that person is unavailable?
  3. Duties: What may I do, what must I not do, and what needs training or direct supervision?
  4. Hazards: What could harm me in my tasks? Which controls must be in place?
  5. Training: What instruction and demonstration are needed before each new task? Who checks that I can do it safely?
  6. Protection: What clothing/PPE is required, who provides it, and how will I learn to use it?
  7. Emergencies: What is the alarm/evacuation procedure, safe exit and meeting point? Who provides first aid? How do I summon urgent help?
  8. Reporting: Who do I tell about hazards, injuries, harassment or concerns? What is the backup route?
  9. Products: If relevant, where are labels/SDSs and who teaches safe product handling? Do not use an unidentified product.
  10. Routines: What are the break, phone, attendance and absence procedures? How do I tell the employer and school about a delay or absence?
  11. Privacy: What information must stay private? What are the rules for photographs, devices and records?
  12. Learning: What safe beginner activity could support my first learning goal? How will I get feedback?

Reference · Paths B and C

Rehearse an orientation.

Read the fictional case. Complete its five tasks in your workbook. These are practice answers, not workplace confirmations.

Cedar Community Centre · fictional case and tasks

TASKS (point form is fine) 1. Sort the note into at least four confirmed fictional facts and four unanswered questions. For each unknown, identify who should answer. 2. Choose three questions to ask before starting tasks, and explain your order. 3. Write the words you would use if asked to move a heavy unidentified box while Jordan is away. Explain the safe next action. 4. Explain why the employee’s photo suggestion is not enough. Propose a privacy-safe way to record learning. 5. Give a 45-second mock introduction, aloud to a partner or as a written script: greet the supervisor, state a learning interest and ask your most important unanswered question. Revise one sentence for clarity.

Finish with a readiness verdict: “I still need confirmation of ___ before ___.”

55–105 min · Independent mission

Complete the First-Day Readiness Mission.

Work through seven mission stations: orientation intel, travel, gear, workplace decisions, a learning goal and your first-day introduction.

Open the First-Day Readiness Mission

  1. Use your teacher-assigned A, B or C route.
  2. Complete all 14 response boxes. Use confirmed facts, or clearly label practice answers and unknowns.
  3. Download your mission file and attach it to the matching Classroom assignment.

The website does not save or submit your work automatically.

105–125 min · Review & conference

Turn gaps into next steps.

A · Debrief your visit

Record what you learned, what remains unclear and which official documents still need attention. Submit any required official records separately. Follow the return/debrief time agreed with your teacher.

B · Finish your rehearsal

Finish the mock tasks. Practise your introduction with a partner or write it independently. Revise one sentence. Identify what the real workplace still needs to confirm.

C · Share your next move

Give your teacher a two-minute update: preferred option, evidence, one barrier and support needed. If the teacher is busy, leave a private Classroom comment and finish your draft. No waiting for the whole block.

125–140 min · Everyone

Make your first-day plan.

  1. Complete the workbook’s First-day plan: start arrangements, reporting location, travel backup, clothing and supplies.
  2. Write what you would say and who you would contact for a delay, unfamiliar duty and injury or urgent concern.
  3. Draft one learning goal with a safe possible activity and observable evidence.
  4. List three unresolved questions with a person and next action.
Example: a useful learning goal

“I want to improve how clearly I explain routine information. After instruction and approval, I could practise a greeting with my supervisor. Evidence: feedback on accuracy and tone, followed by a revised greeting.”

If information is missing, say so. Discuss personal support needs privately. A classmate’s answers do not confirm your workplace arrangements.

140–145 min · Check

Check your readiness thinking.

Complete Day 5 — Readiness Check in Classroom: six short questions. Read feedback and revisit any errors. This is a learning check, not placement clearance.

If the Form is unavailable, use the practice check and tell your teacher. Practice results are not submitted.

145–150 min · Together & submit

Finish with one clear next step.

  1. Turn in Day 5 — Orientation & First-Day Plan. Complete only your assigned pathway plus the shared sections.
  2. Complete Day 5 — Completion Check.
  3. Return official documents separately using the teacher’s private instructions.
  4. Identify your next action in the wrap-up. If you are still offsite, use the individual arrangement agreed with your teacher.

Your teacher confirms when you may start placement. Tomorrow: CO-OP Desk, daily logs, weekly sign-off and final preparation.
