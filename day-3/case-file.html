Workplace Response Case File.

Separate facts from assumptions. Choose a safe response. Protect privacy.

Case route 1 · Choose a file2 · Sort evidence3 · Identify concerns4 · Build response5 · Write report6 · Compare7 · Export 0 of 12 responses started
Time: Approx. 8 minutes.

Choose one case file.

Read all three. Select one for the full investigation. All people and workplaces are fictional. Do not add a personal incident.

FILE A

Same instructions for everyone

Jordan tells the co-op teacher privately that written steps help with a disability-related learning need. The teacher and Jordan plan to discuss appropriate support with the placement. At orientation, supervisor Pat says, “Everyone gets verbal instructions. Special treatment is unfair.” Pat assigns Jordan a multi-step stock task and leaves. A coworker says Pat is usually impatient. No written workplace accommodation response or alternate contact has been identified.

FILE B

Messages after placement

Supervisor Lee sends Morgan several unwanted messages about Morgan’s appearance after placement hours. One says that going on a date could lead to “better opportunities.” Morgan replies once: “Please keep messages about work.” Lee sends another comment. A coworker says Lee is “probably joking.” The workplace policy says concerns about a supervisor can be reported to the manager. Morgan has saved the messages and has not replied again.

FILE C

The photo and the cover-up

Taylor takes a selfie at a clinic desk. A patient’s name and appointment time are visible on a screen behind Taylor. Taylor sends it to two friends. Coworker Robin says, “Delete it and nobody needs to know,” then suggests uploading it to a public AI tool to blur the screen. The clinic’s orientation prohibited photos and identified the privacy lead as an alternate contact. Taylor has not posted the photo publicly.

Time: Approx. 10 minutes.

Sort the evidence.

Build the response ladder.

Time: Approx. 8 minutes.

Write a factual private report.

Write 100–150 words to an appropriate contact. Include the known facts, immediate action, what help is requested and a follow-up question. Do not diagnose, exaggerate, promise secrecy, assign legal guilt or include invented details.

Time: Approx. 5 minutes.

Compare and transfer.

Time: Approx. 3 minutes.

Check and submit.

  • Five neutral facts, two assumptions and three unknowns
  • Concern identified from evidence, with uncertainty where appropriate
  • Safe first action and backup reporting route
  • Private information kept out of public/class channels
  • 100–150 word factual report
  • One comparison and transferable rule
