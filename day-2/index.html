Learning goals & success criteria

Learn to research a placement or career interest, connect your strengths to work, and plan your next steps.

I can:
  • Use two reliable sources and distinguish facts from assumptions.
  • Connect three strengths to workplace tasks using real examples.
  • Identify what needs teacher or supervisor confirmation.
  • Create a practical goal, useful questions and a clear introduction.

2. Learn how to research.

Part 1 of Placement Preview & Success Plan. Write your research here once. Section 3 adds your strengths and preparation plan; it will not ask you to retype these notes. Both parts are included in one workbook download.

  1. Start with the workplace. Look for its services, location and who it serves. If you are on Path B, compare two types of organization.
  2. Explore the occupation. Open Job Bank career planning, choose job profiles or occupation exploration, search a job title, and check duties and requirements. Use Ontario or your region where relevant.
  3. Record the source beside the claim. Save the page title, publisher, URL and date checked. Use the exact page address, not a search-results page.
  4. Label the unknowns. Write “to confirm” beside student duties, schedule, training or clothing that a source does not establish. Add who you will ask.
Example: fact, assumption or question?

Fact: the organization’s website says it repairs bicycles. Assumption: a student will repair every bicycle independently. Question: “Which tasks could I do, and what training would I receive?” This example is fictional.

A job profile describes an occupation. It does not authorize you to perform every task listed. Keep professional duties separate from possible student learning tasks.

Use at least two sources: an official occupation/pathway source and an employer, professional organization or education provider. If Job Bank is unavailable, use the official pathway links below plus a relevant organization’s page. No account or employer contact is required.

Research checklist and helpful links
  • Describe the workplace, whom it serves and its work environment.
  • Research the occupation, duties, tools and a relevant training pathway.
  • Identify clothing, training, transportation or paperwork questions that need confirmation.
  • Save publisher, page title, exact URL and date checked for each source.
  • Check that each source supports your claim.

Ontario Colleges · Ontario Universities’ Info · Ontario apprenticeships

Help with strengths, goals and your introduction

Watch the Skills for Success overview or read its transcript.

Strength: “I showed ___ when I ___. At this workplace, that could help me ___.”

Goal: name one skill, two safe actions, how you will notice improvement and who could help.

Introduction: say who you are, what you want to learn, a strength with an example, one fact about the workplace and a useful question. Revise one vague phrase. Do not send it or promise a start date.

If you finish early, compare another training route or use a new source to improve your plan.

Placement Match Challenge

Google Classroom: Placement Match Challenge

Separate assignment: fictional matching activity. This does not use Path A or Path B. Everyone reads the same four fictional student profiles and six workplace cards, then recommends three matches using evidence.

Open the Placement Match Challenge ↗

  1. Read: student profiles and workplace cards.
  2. Match: make three recommendations using specific evidence, possible challenges and questions that still need answers.
  3. Investigate: find at least four red flags in one workplace offer and explain the professional next step.
  4. Transfer: rank your own placement priorities and improve one question for a teacher or supervisor.
  5. Submit: download your responses and attach them to Placement Match Challenge in Classroom.

The activity is designed for about 50 minutes. It does not autosave or submit your work. Download before closing or reloading.
