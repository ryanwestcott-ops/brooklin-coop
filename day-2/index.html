Find your direction.

Understand your placement. Prepare to succeed.

Time: Approx. 5 minutes.

Choose your route.

Today: investigate a workplace, connect your strengths to the work, and prepare a useful first-week plan.

Route A

You have a confirmed or likely placement. Research that organization and a relevant occupation.

Route B

Your placement is undecided. Choose an occupation or sector that interests you and explore two types of workplace.

I can…

  • Use reliable sources and separate facts from assumptions.
  • Connect three strengths to workplace tasks using examples.
  • Plan practical next steps and useful supervisor questions.

Open Day 2 — Placement Preview & Success Plan in Classroom. Complete one route in your own copy. Point form is welcome.

Time: Approx. 15 minutes.

Research without guessing.

  1. Start with the workplace. Look for its services, location and who it serves. If you are on Route B, compare two types of organization.
  2. Explore the occupation. Open Job Bank career planning, choose job profiles or occupation exploration, search a job title, and check duties and requirements. Use Ontario or your region where relevant.
  3. Record the source beside the claim. Save the page title, publisher, URL and date checked. Link the exact page, not a search-results page.
  4. Label the unknowns. Write “to confirm” beside student duties, schedule, training or clothing that a source does not establish. Add who you will ask.
Example: fact, assumption or question?

Fact: the organization’s website says it repairs bicycles. Assumption: a student will repair every bicycle independently. Question: “Which tasks could I do, and what training would I receive?” This example is fictional.

A job profile describes an occupation. It does not authorize you to perform every task listed. Keep professional duties separate from possible student learning tasks.

Use at least two sources: an official occupation/pathway source and an employer, professional organization or education provider. If Job Bank is unavailable, use the official pathway links below plus a relevant organization’s page. No account or employer contact is required.

Time: Approx. 30 minutes.

Build your placement preview.

Begin workbook sections 1–2. Spend about 10 minutes on the organization, 10 on the occupation/pathway, and 10 on tools, requirements and source notes. Mark unfinished details “continue after challenge” instead of inventing answers.

  1. A — Confirmed or likely placement: name the organization, website and location. What does it do, whom does it serve, and what is the work environment? Separate facts on the website from questions you still need to ask.
  2. B — Still exploring: choose an occupation or sector and two types of organization where that work occurs. Explain what they do, whom they serve and their work environments. You do not need to find vacancies or contact employers.
  3. Both routes: list five duties from an occupation profile. For each, label it: possible student task to confirm / observation only to confirm / trained professional responsibility. Explain one boundary. An occupation profile is not permission to perform a task.
  4. Name three tools, technologies or specialized terms and explain their use in your own words. Identify expected clothing and working conditions from a source, or write “to confirm” where unknown.
  5. Identify three initial requirements or preparation needs. For each, record what you know, what you need to confirm, whom you will ask and one next action. Consider clothing, training, transportation and paperwork.
  1. Give the occupation title and link its Job Bank profile or another reliable occupation source. Identify two important skills and one possible education or training route. Distinguish career-entry qualifications from your co-op preparation. Note whether apprenticeship is relevant; confirm using an official source rather than guessing.
  2. Find one trend or change in the occupation (for example, a new technology or a change in service). Link the source and explain one way it could affect your learning or future planning. If evidence is unavailable, write a research question instead of inventing a trend.
  3. Use at least two identifiable sources. For each, give publisher, page title, exact URL, date checked, and one claim it supports. Include one official occupation/pathway source and one employer, professional organization or education provider source. Add one “to confirm” question.
Education and pathway links
  • Ontario Colleges — explore programs
  • Ontario Universities’ Info — explore programs
  • Ontario apprenticeship information

Follow the route relevant to your occupation. You do not need to research every pathway or apply to a program.

Time: Approx. 10 minutes.

Can you support your claims?

Review your research before moving on. In your workbook, highlight one sourced fact and one question still to confirm.

  • Do both links open to the pages I used?
  • Does each source support the claim beside it?
  • Have I separated occupation duties from possible student duties?
  • Have I explained why one discovery matters for my preparation?

Fix one weak or unsupported claim. If stuck, mark the question and ask your teacher.

In your workbook, rewrite one unsupported claim as a question you can investigate. Check both source links and save your work.

Time: Approx. 15 minutes.

Show what a strength looks like.

A strength becomes useful when you can describe what you do. “I’m organized” becomes “I kept our team’s materials labelled so everyone could find them.” Then explain how that behaviour could help at your placement.

Watch “Discover the Skills for Success” or read its transcript. Choose one skill and connect it to a behaviour in your setting. The transcript is on the same page.

You might draw on communication, numeracy, digital skills, problem solving, adaptability, collaboration, creativity, reading or writing. Reliability, empathy and attention to detail can also be useful strengths to discuss.

  1. Choose three strengths. For each: describe a specific example from your own experience and explain how it could help in this setting. You may extend your Day 1 example and add two others. School, home, hobbies, work and community examples count.
  2. Watch the Skills for Success overview or read its transcript. Choose one skill and describe a behaviour that would show it in your setting. Link it to one of your examples above.
Sentence starter

“I showed ___ when I ___. At this placement, that could help me ___.” Use your own example, not a personality label.

Time: Approx. 50 minutes.

Placement Match Challenge.

You are the co-op placement adviser. Read four fictional student profiles and six workplace cards. Make three evidence-based matches, identify workplace concerns and apply the criteria to your own search.

Open the Placement Match Challenge ↗

  1. Read: student profiles and workplace cards.
  2. Match: make three recommendations using specific evidence, possible challenges and questions that still need answers.
  3. Investigate: find at least four red flags in one workplace offer and explain the professional next step.
  4. Transfer: rank your own placement priorities and improve one question for a teacher or supervisor.
  5. Submit: download or copy your responses and attach them to Day 2 — Placement Match Challenge in Classroom.

The activity is designed for about 50 minutes. It does not autosave or submit your work. Download before closing or reloading.

Time: Approx. 10 minutes.

Make your first week easier.

Use your challenge decisions to begin workbook section 4. Build on Day 1 rather than starting the same goal again. Complete remaining research or planning if you finish early.

  1. Choose one skill or work habit to improve. Explain why it matters here. Name two practical actions, evidence of improvement and a support person. Build on or revise your Day 1 goal.
  2. Complete these first-week prompts: arrival and backup transportation; what to bring/wear (confirm before buying); a two-sentence introduction; what to do if instructions are unclear; what to do when a task is finished; how to remember new learning without recording private workplace details; how to respond to feedback. Detailed daily logging is on Day 6.
  3. Write three useful questions for your supervisor or placement interview. Include one about tasks/training, one about routines and one about feedback or support. Do not send them today.
  4. Finish: one discovery that increased your interest; one concern or uncertainty; one action you have already taken today to prepare, with evidence such as a draft question or saved route.
Make a question useful

Instead of “What do I do?”, try “After I finish an assigned task, who should I check with?” Instead of “Am I good?”, try “What is one thing I could do more clearly next time?”

Already finished? Compare a second training route or improve your plan using a new source. Do not contact an employer or buy equipment for this activity.

Time: Approx. 5 minutes.

Introduce yourself with evidence.

Add the heading Introduction rehearsal at the end of your workbook. Use the research and examples you already have.

  1. Draft: write a concise introduction for a possible supervisor. Include who you are, what you want to learn, one strength with a real example, one specific connection to this workplace and a useful question. Do not promise a start date or claim skills you do not have.
  2. Review: read it aloud quietly or review it silently. Mark one vague phrase and improve it. You do not need to record yourself or find a partner.
  3. Revise: use one feedback point below to improve your introduction.
  4. Respond: if time permits, write a two-sentence answer to “What would you do if you did not understand a task?”
Feedback to apply
  • Replace “I am hardworking” with one brief example of something you did.
  • Replace “I want experience” with a skill or process you want to understand.
  • Check that your workplace detail is supported by a source.
  • Replace a promise to work independently with willingness to learn through instruction and supervision.
  • Use words you would actually say. Do not copy an example or send the introduction today.

Finished early? Adapt your introduction for a different type of workplace. Identify what changed and what stayed useful. Add this extension to the same workbook.

Time: Approx. 5 minutes.

Check your preparation.

  2. Turn in your Placement Preview & Success Plan Google Doc.
  3. Complete Day 2 — Completion Check. Report a blocker honestly if something remains unfinished.

If a Form is unavailable, use the practice version and tell your teacher. If the workbook is unavailable, copy the workbook prompts into a private Doc.
