05 · Apply · 75–115 min · Independent
Turn an interest into a first step.
Continue in
Day 1 — Learner Profile & First Goal
. Your placement can be undecided; use an area of interest. Spend about 10 minutes exploring ideas, 20 minutes drafting and 10 minutes checking your response.
A. Write your first learning goal
Interest:
What kind of workplace or task would you like to understand better? Why?
Goal:
What specific skill would you like to improve during your first two weeks at an approved placement?
Action:
What is one safe first step you could take, with your supervisor's guidance?
Evidence:
What could you notice or receive feedback on that would show progress?
Support:
Who could help, and what question would you ask?
See an example — then write your own
“I want to learn how a recreation centre welcomes visitors. During my first two weeks, I want to give a clear greeting and know when to refer a question. I will ask to observe my supervisor, then practise an approved greeting. I will ask for feedback on clarity and when I sought help. My question is: ‘Which questions should I always pass to you?’”
B. Practise a professional message
A supervisor emails: “We'd love to have you start tomorrow afternoon.” Your teacher has not cleared your placement. Draft a short reply with a subject line, greeting, thanks, explanation that you need teacher confirmation, a question about the next step, and a closing.
Do not send it.
Need a starting sentence?
“Thank you for the opportunity. I am looking forward to learning with your team. Before I confirm a start date, I need to…”
Before you submit:
Is the goal specific? Is your action safe and realistic? Could you recognize progress? Does your message avoid promising a start date?
