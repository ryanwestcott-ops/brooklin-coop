02 · Learn · 10–30 min · Independent

Co-op is a course in the real world.

Current course clarifications — read with the presentation
  • Our logs are daily, with Friday review and signatures. Detailed practice comes on Day 6.
  • Your supervisor provides feedback; your teacher evaluates course achievement.
  • Confidentiality never means keeping safety concerns secret. Report concerns to the appropriate workplace and school contacts.
  • Use Classroom for the actual required forms and deadlines; do not assume the slide's “tomorrow” applies.
  • OYAP eligibility has changed from the slide's 16/16 statement. Read the current DDSB OYAP information and ask your teacher about your situation.

In DCO3O9, you learn through experience in a community placement. You will prepare, participate, reflect and use what you learn to decide what to try next. Your interests can change as you discover more.

Your part

Attend, arrive prepared, ask questions, follow instructions and complete your learning tasks. Tell your teacher when you need support.

Your support team

Your teacher plans and monitors your learning. Your workplace supervisor guides your work and gives feedback. Stay in contact with both.

Know these four essentials

  1. Prepare before you go. Your teacher must confirm your placement, required paperwork, preparation and approved schedule before you start. An employer's invitation alone is not clearance.
  2. Follow the calendar. Pre-placement is September 9–16 on the six school days listed on the home page. September 17 is the planned first placement day, subject to individual approval. Integration days bring you back to school.
  3. Be reliable and communicate. Brooklin's two-credit placement schedule requires at least 2.5 hours on each scheduled placement day. Follow your approved schedule. If absent, notify your employer, co-op teacher and school attendance before your start time, or as soon as possible in an emergency.
  4. Ask before guessing. If instructions are unclear or a task feels unsafe, pause and ask your supervisor. Contact your teacher for concerns or support. Keep workplace and personal information private.

Today's 150 minutes are classroom learning. The 2.5-hour placement rule applies when you attend your approved workplace. Detailed check-in, daily log and Friday sign-off practice comes on Day 6.

03 · Check · 30–40 min · Independent

What would you do?

Try all three situations. Read the feedback, explain why the other action would cause a problem, then correct your thinking. These are practice checks, not submissions.

1. “Can you start tomorrow?”

A placement supervisor offers you a start date, but your teacher has not confirmed it.

2. You are too ill to attend.

3. Which statement gives evidence?

Write one sentence explaining each decision. Ask your teacher privately if you still have a question.

04 · Apply · 40–60 min · Independent

Tell your teacher about you.

Open Day 1 — Learner Profile & First Goal in Google Classroom. Complete the profile section first. If the Form isn't assigned yet, draft the answers in a private Google Doc using the prompts below.

  1. What name would you like your teacher to use?
  2. What is your placement status: exploring options, contacting a workplace, interview arranged, or placement awaiting teacher clearance?
  3. Which two kinds of work interest you? What would you like to discover about them?
  4. Name a strength. Describe one time you used it at school, home, work, in a hobby or in your community.
  5. What helps you learn a new task: a demonstration, written steps, supervised practice, a chance to ask questions, or something else?
  6. Would you like a private conversation about placement, transportation, access to technology or learning support? You can request one without explaining personal details here.

Do not enter medical details, passwords, home addresses or other people's personal information. Discuss sensitive needs directly with your teacher.

05 · Apply · 60–75 min + 110–125 min · Independent

Turn an interest into a first step.

Continue in Day 1 — Learner Profile & First Goal. Your placement can be undecided; use an area of interest. Use 60–75 minutes to draft your goal. At the start of Period 2, complete the interest lab below. Return here at 110–125 minutes to improve your goal and finish your message.

A. Write your first learning goal

  1. Interest: What kind of workplace or task would you like to understand better? Why?
  2. Goal: What specific skill would you like to improve during your first two weeks at an approved placement?
  3. Action: What is one safe first step you could take, with your supervisor's guidance?
  4. Evidence: What could you notice or receive feedback on that would show progress?
  5. Support: Who could help, and what question would you ask?
See an example — then write your own

“I want to learn how a recreation centre welcomes visitors. During my first two weeks, I want to give a clear greeting and know when to refer a question. I will ask to observe my supervisor, then practise an approved greeting. I will ask for feedback on clarity and when I sought help. My question is: ‘Which questions should I always pass to you?’”

B. Practise a professional message

A supervisor emails: “We'd love to have you start tomorrow afternoon.” Your teacher has not cleared your placement. Draft a short reply with a subject line, greeting, thanks, explanation that you need teacher confirmation, a question about the next step, and a closing. Do not send it.

Need a starting sentence?

“Thank you for the opportunity. I am looking forward to learning with your team. Before I confirm a start date, I need to…”

Before you submit: Is the goal specific? Is your action safe and realistic? Could you recognize progress? Does your message avoid promising a start date?

Period 2 · 75–110 min · Independent

Test-drive your interests.

Could a workplace that sounds interesting actually suit the way you like to learn? Compare fictional placements, look for evidence and decide what you would need to find out.

Open the 35-minute interest lab ↗

  1. Read three workplace snapshots and choose two to compare.
  2. Explain a task that appeals, a challenge and a strength you could bring.
  3. Choose a next question and revise your initial impression.
  4. Download your response and attach it to Day 1 — Test-drive Your Interests in Classroom.

Then return to your first-step plan above for 110–125 minutes. Use one insight from the lab to improve your goal.

06 · Organize · 125–140 min · Independent

Make tomorrow easier.

  1. Find the current course procedures and required signature forms in Google Classroom. Read them and note any questions for your teacher. Follow the return instructions on that assignment; today's learner profile is not a permission form.
  2. Save your teacher's contact information: the teacher contact information in Google Classroom. Find the school attendance contact in your school communication system.
  3. Check the semester calendar. Add the first integration day to your planner.
  4. Check that you can open your Classroom assignments with your DDSB account. Tell your teacher about access difficulties.

If required school forms have not been posted yet, note that for your teacher and finish your learning work. If you're done early, write a second possible goal and explain which goal you would prioritize.

07 · Submit · 140–145 min · Independent

Leave with a clear next step.

Attach your lab response to Day 1 — Test-drive Your Interests. Submit your profile, goal and practice message in Day 1 — Learner Profile & First Goal. Then complete Day 1 — Exit Check in Classroom:

  1. What must happen before you start placement?
  2. What is one action you will take before the next class?
  3. What question do you still have, or what support would help?

This checklist is a reminder only. It resets when you reload and does not submit or report work to your teacher.

145–150 min · Wrap-up together (5 min). Be ready to name your next action. Your teacher will explain tomorrow's focus and handle questions.
