Your task

Compare two fictional settings and explain what you want to explore. There is no best career or personality score. These snapshots are classroom examples, not available placements or permission to perform tasks.

Time: Approx. 35 minutes.

1 · First impression

2 · Read at least three snapshots

Read all four if you are undecided. Then compare two. You do not need to research employers today; that comes on Day 2.

Recreation centre

You could observe how staff welcome visitors, prepare activity supplies and explain routines after instruction. The setting can be busy and noisy. You would need to ask when a question should be passed to a staff member. A possible learning focus is clear communication. Enjoying sports does not automatically mean you enjoy customer questions.

Automotive workplace

You could observe how a team organizes a service job and, after approval and instruction, help with suitable preparation tasks. The environment may involve noise, standing and close attention to procedures. Tools and equipment require specific training and authorization. A possible learning focus is following a sequence accurately. Liking cars does not automatically mean you enjoy detailed checking.

School office

You could observe how staff organize requests and prepare non-confidential materials under direction. Tasks may involve interruptions, careful checking and repeated routines. Private student information must stay within approved systems. A possible learning focus is organization. Enjoying a quiet desk task does not mean every office day is quiet.

Community garden

You could observe how staff plan seasonal work and assist with approved tasks after instruction. Conditions and plans may change with weather. You may need to ask about tools, safe handling and the daily priorities. A possible learning focus is adapting a plan. Enjoying the outdoors does not mean every task or weather condition will suit you.
