Day 4: Workplace Safety & WHMIS

Learning goals

Recognize hazards, choose stronger controls and find the safety information you need before a task.

Success criteria

  • I can identify possible harm and explain which concern needs attention first.
  • I can use labels and safety data sheets (SDSs) to find information.
  • I can explain when to stop and ask, and prepare specific orientation questions.

Hazards & WHMIS

Read the notes and open the references as needed. Try both practice questions, then use this section while completing the interactive challenges.

Spot the hazard, then consider the risk

A hazard can cause harm. To consider the risk, ask who could be exposed, how serious the harm could be and how likely it is. Missing information is something to confirm.

Look for hazards involving equipment, electricity, noise, heat, chemicals, infection exposure, awkward movement or threats. More than one type can appear in the same task.

Choose stronger controls

  1. Elimination: remove the hazard.
  2. Substitution: use a safer option, checking for new hazards.
  3. Engineering: use design, guards or barriers.
  4. Administrative: use training, procedures and supervision.
  5. PPE: use correctly selected protective equipment.

Combine controls as needed. Recommend improvements to responsible staff; do not repair equipment or change a work process yourself.

Try it 1: A cord crosses a busy walkway. Which proposal addresses the hazard most directly?

Find the product information

WHMIS uses labels, SDSs and education/training to communicate hazardous-product information.

  • Supplier labels: look for the product and supplier identification, hazard and precautionary information, and applicable signal words and pictograms.
  • Workplace labels: generally identify the product, safe-handling information and the SDS reference.
  • Pictograms: flag hazards. A symbol alone does not tell you the full safe procedure.
