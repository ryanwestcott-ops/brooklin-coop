0–4 minutes

Your assignment

You are reviewing fictional workplaces before a co-op student begins. Choose two scenes for a detailed investigation. Recommend what responsible workplace staff should examine—not repairs a student should perform.

This is classroom practice. A text scene cannot prove that a real workplace is safe. Mark unknown information and ask for workplace-specific training before doing a task.

4–12 minutes

Examine the scene cards.

SCENE 1

Busy café prep area

  • A delivery box partly blocks the marked exit route.
  • A worker carries a full pot around the box.
  • A wet-floor sign is folded under a counter while water is visible near the sink.
  • A spray bottle has a product name but no visible safe-handling information or SDS reference.
  • The student has been shown the staff washroom but not the first-aid location.
SCENE 2

Automotive service bay

  • An extension cord crosses a walking route.
  • A vehicle is raised; the student has not been told the exclusion area.
  • A container is labelled “shop mix” with no other readable information.
  • Safety glasses are available, but no one has explained when or how they are required.
  • A machine guard appears loose. Someone says, “Just avoid that side.”
SCENE 3

Elementary classroom

  • Backpacks narrow a route to the doorway.
  • A student is asked to clean an unfamiliar bodily-fluid spill.
  • A rolling cart is stacked high enough to block the person pushing it.
  • The co-op student does not know the school’s lockdown or evacuation signal.
  • A staff member offers disposable gloves but gives no cleanup procedure.
SCENE 4

Retail stockroom

  • Heavy boxes are stored above shoulder height.
  • A step ladder has a damaged foot and no inspection tag is visible.
  • An emergency exit is clear, but the assembly point is unknown.
  • A worker suggests climbing a shelf for a light item.
  • The student is asked to use a compactor but has received no task training.

24–32 minutes

Build stronger controls.

For the top hazard in each scene, consider elimination, substitution, engineering controls, administrative controls and PPE—in that order. More than one control may be needed.

32–37 minutes

Open the WHMIS evidence file.

A student finds a spray bottle labelled only “shop mix.” A coworker says it is probably the usual cleaner and hands over gloves. No matching supplier/workplace label or SDS has been confirmed.

37–42 minutes

Speak up professionally.

42–45 minutes

Review, export and submit.

  • Two different scenes and four hazards in each
  • Two justified priorities
  • Controls considered above PPE
  • No student-directed repair, cleanup or equipment experiment
  • Correct response to unidentified product information
  • Professional stop message and three orientation questions
